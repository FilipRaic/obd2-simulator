# Gerber and drill files

The ordering procedure written for a first-time builder is on the **Building the
device** page of the repository wiki.
These files are regenerated by
[`../kicad/scripts/regen_outputs.ps1`](../kicad/scripts/README.md), never
exported by hand.

Fabrication files for the dedicated board, **revision v0.7**, two-layer FR4,
**130 x 115 mm**, exported from `../kicad/obd2-simulator.kicad_pcb`
(KiCad 10, `kicad-cli`). Last export: 11.08.2026.

Board state at that export: 107 footprints, 64 nets including ground, 892 track
segments, 73 vias, DRC clean with nothing unconnected. The drill file holds
**326 holes** in total. The smallest hole is **0,30 mm** (93 of them, 72 vias
and 21 holes inside footprints), and copper clearance sits exactly on the
0,150 mm rule everywhere.

The vias are not all alike: 72 are 0,60 / 0,30 mm and one is 0,80 / 0,40 mm, on
net V5_STICK, because that net belongs to the `Power` class.

## Why no hole on the board is smaller than 0,30 mm

Until 11.08.2026. there were twelve of them, **0,20 mm** across, all in the same
place: thermal vias under pad 41 (EPAD) of the U6 module footprint. They were
not chosen but inherited, because KiCad's `ESP32-S3-WROOM-1` footprint ships
that way. They are the reason the project's `min_through_hole_diameter` rule
stood at 0,20 mm.

Those holes were widened to 0,30 mm, because JLCPCB bills a drill under 0,30 mm
as an advanced parameter and they were the only item pushing the order into that
class. The change makes nothing worse:

| Item | Before | After | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hole | 0,20 mm | 0,30 mm | 0,30 mm standard |
| Pad | 0,60 mm | 0,60 mm, unchanged | - |
| Annular ring | 0,20 mm | 0,15 mm | 0,13 mm |
| Edge-to-edge gap between neighbouring holes in the grid | 0,79 mm | 0,69 mm | 0,25 mm (board rule) |

Thermally it is an improvement, since a wider wall means more copper between the
module pad and the ground pour. No pad moved, so the board was not re-routed:
the hole size was changed directly in the `.kicad_pcb`, and DRC still reports 0
errors and 0 unconnected items. To stop the library footprint from slipping back
in, `build_board.py` now raises every hole of every loaded footprint to
`MIN_DRILL_MM = 0,3`, and the design rule stands at the same value.

## Hole-to-hole spacing is under 0,50 mm, and across different nets

Worth knowing **before** picking a fabricator. In its standard two-layer process
JLCPCB asks for 0,50 mm between holes belonging to different nets. The board
does not meet that, measured on 10.08.2026. by running DRC with the rule raised
to 0,50 mm:

| Pair | Spacing | Nets |
|---|---|---|
| 15 pairs inside footprint J1 (USB-C) | **0,450 mm** (14 at 0,4500 and one at 0,4524) | different (GND, VBUS, CC, D+, D-) |
| vias `RXD0` and `TXD0` | **0,4694 mm** | different |
| 6 thermal vias under the exposed pad of U1 | 0,400 mm | same (ground) |

Twenty-two pairs in total. The measurement was repeated on 11.08.2026., after
the vias under module U6 were widened to 0,30 mm, and produced an **identical
list**. Drill diameter and hole spacing are two independent parameters and have
to be checked separately when ordering.

Those 15 pairs come from **the official footprint itself**,
`USB_C_Receptacle_GCT_USB4085`, so that is the geometry of the connector and
cannot be fixed without changing the part. The board therefore needs a
fabricator who accepts 0,45 mm anyway, and one who does also passes the via pair
at 0,466 mm. Check that when ordering, or ask up front.

Note: DRC against the project rules reports 0 errors because the project rule is
set to 0,25 mm. That is not an oversight but a consequence of the connector
footprint being what it is.

## The package to upload

**`obd2-simulator-v07-for-fabrication.zip`** is what goes to the fabricator's web
form. It holds exactly nine fabrication files, without the auxiliary layers
(Courtyard, Fab, Adhesive, Margin, User_*, Eco, Comments, Drawings), because
some readers pick those up as extra copper or as an outline.

It is built by `../kicad/scripts/make_fab_zip.py`, and `regen_outputs.ps1`
refreshes it together with the other derived files.

The paste layers are deliberately left out: the board is hand-soldered and no
steel stencil is ordered. If one is ever needed, add `F_Paste.gtp` and
`B_Paste.gbp`.

The ordering procedure, the parameters to enter and the checks to run before
uploading are described in chapter 1 of the technical documentation.

| File | Layer |
|---|---|
| `obd2-simulator-F_Cu.gtl` | copper, top layer |
| `obd2-simulator-B_Cu.gbl` | copper, bottom layer (GND pour) |
| `obd2-simulator-F_Mask.gts` / `B_Mask.gbs` | solder mask |
| `obd2-simulator-F_Paste.gtp` / `B_Paste.gbp` | paste (SMD stencil) |
| `obd2-simulator-F_Silkscreen.gto` / `B_Silkscreen.gbo` | silkscreen |
| `obd2-simulator-Edge_Cuts.gm1` | board outline |
| `obd2-simulator.drl` | drill (Excellon) |
| `obd2-simulator-job.gbrjob` | Gerber job file |

> **Before ordering fabrication** read `../README.md` and
> `../kicad/PRE-FABRICATION-CHECKLIST.md`: the custom footprints (CH224K,
> SKRHABE010, SEP-A-OBD-D2, EN11-HSB1AQ20) and the mechanical fit of the
> connectors are verified against the parts as delivered. Re-export the files
> after those checks.
>
> Note: the paste layer (`F_Paste`) now covers the integrated circuits only,
> since every passive is through-hole, so a stencil is worth ordering only if
> the ICs are to be paste-soldered.
